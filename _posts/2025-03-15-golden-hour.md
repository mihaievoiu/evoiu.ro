## The Magic Window

There's a reason photographers obsess over the golden hour. That brief period after sunrise and before sunset bathes the world in warm, directional light that flatters almost any subject — and transforms the mundane into something remarkable.

I've spent years chasing this window, and I've learned that success comes down to three things: preparation, flexibility, and patience.

## Know Before You Go

The first rule is simple: know exactly when and where the sun will be. Apps like PhotoPills and The Photographer's Ephemeris show you precisely where the sun rises and sets at your location, and how the light will fall. I spend ten minutes planning for every hour I'm shooting.

The second rule: arrive early. Golden hour doesn't wait. Conditions can change in minutes — a cloud bank rolls in, the light turns flat — and if you're still setting up when the best light hits, you've missed it.

{% include cloudinary_img.html src="https://res.cloudinary.com/dblfokmtd/image/upload/v1777033624/backyard-color-36_fq9cdq.jpg" alt="" sizes="(max-width: 740px) 100vw, 740px" %}

## Work the Scene

Once you're in position, resist the urge to shoot everything at once. Pick one composition and stay with it as the light changes. The difference between fifteen minutes before golden hour and peak golden hour can be dramatic. Let the light come to you.

Look for long shadows — they add depth and drama. Look for how the warm light rakes across surfaces, revealing texture that's invisible at midday.

## Embrace the Unexpected

My best golden-hour shots rarely come from my planned compositions. They come from something I notice on the way to the shot — a person walking through the frame, a cloud catching light in an unexpected way, a reflection in a shop window.

> The plan is a starting point. Presence is everything.

Show up. Stay alert. Let the light surprise you.
